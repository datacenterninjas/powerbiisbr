Your first day as a Business Analyst Intern

"Attached is our raw sales data. Board meeting is tomorrow morning. I need a one-page dashboard, and I need YOU to walk me through 3 insights before I present." — that's the whole brief. That's also almost word-for-word what a real first assignment looks like.

The email, in full: "Hi — I'm Ananya Rao, VP of Sales here at Meridian Retail. Attached is our raw sales export, straight out of the source system — nobody's touched it. Board meeting is 9am tomorrow. I need a one-page dashboard on my laptop by end of day, and I need YOU to walk me through 3 insights before I present them as mine. Don't send me a data dump — send me the 3 things I should actually say out loud." That's the whole brief, and every deliverable below exists because Ananya asked for it, not because a rubric did.

Milestones (don't leave this to the last night)

CheckpointDue byYou should be able to show

Nobody checks these three dates for you — they exist so "I'll start the week it's due" doesn't collide with a board meeting.

Deliverable checklist

  • ✓
    Data imported from Sales_Dirty.csv
  • ✓
    All data-quality issues cleaned in Power Query — zero errors, zero unexplained blanks, zero duplicate rows
  • ✓
    At least 4 KPI Cards: Total Sales, Total Profit, Total Orders, Average Order Value
  • ✓
    At least 4 chart types, each answering a clear business question
  • ✓
    Consistent theme, real titles, interactions tested
  • ✓
    3 spoken insights, each backed by a specific number

The insight template

Every insight follows this exact sentence structure:

"[Business fact] is happening in [specific area], shown by [specific number/chart], which suggests [business implication]."

Model example: "Electronics is our strongest category, generating roughly ₹9.2 lakh — nearly a third of total sales — shown by the tallest bar in our Category chart, which suggests marketing spend should keep prioritizing Electronics inventory ahead of the next quarter."

DAX you'll need

Average Order Value = DIVIDE(SUM(Sales[Sales]), DISTINCTCOUNT(Sales[Order ID]))
Before writing any DAX: rename your table to Sales right after loading (right-click the query name → Rename). Every formula on this site assumes that name — a table still called "Sheet1" will show a red error, not because the formula is wrong, but because the name doesn't match.

Grading rubric

Five categories, not the usual "mechanics first" split — Business Insight & Narrative ties for the highest weight on this rubric, on purpose. A perfectly clean model with nothing to say about it is not what Ananya asked for.

CriterionWeight
Data preparation — cleaned correctly in Power Query, reproducible steps15%
Model & measures — all 4 KPIs written as real DAX measures, correctly aggregating20%
Visualization & design — 4+ chart types, consistent theme, real titles, readable labels25%
Business insight & narrative — 3 insights, each backed by a specific number, each one Ananya could say out loud unedited25%
Delivery — milestones met, submission format followed exactly, presentation-ready15%

How to submit

Two files, exact naming, both to the LMS portal:
  1. Your Power BI file, submitted as YourFullName_FinalProject.pbix
  2. A single PDF containing a full-page screenshot of your dashboard, plus your 3 insights written out in full using the template above (not just bullet points — the exact sentences Ananya would say), submitted as YourFullName_FinalProject_Insights.pdf

The "spoken" insights need a written record somewhere, or there's nothing to grade after the fact — the PDF is that record. If your course also includes a live presentation, this PDF is what you'd be presenting from, not a replacement for it.
