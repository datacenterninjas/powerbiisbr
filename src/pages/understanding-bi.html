Before Power BI: what "Business Intelligence" actually means

Every tool-specific lesson on this site sits on top of one idea — turning raw operational data into decisions a business can act on. Worth naming explicitly before diving into any one tool.

This page is a placeholder — full lesson content lands here as the class reaches this module. The framing below is real; the guided walkthrough, exercises, and quiz questions are still to come.

The idea in one sentence

Business Intelligence (BI) is the practice of collecting data a business already generates — sales, visits, support tickets, inventory — and turning it into something a human can look at and make a decision from, instead of it sitting unused in a database or spreadsheet.

Kitchen analogy carries over: BI as a discipline is the whole meal — sourcing ingredients, cooking, plating, serving. Power BI (the tool this course teaches) is one kitchen you could cook that meal in; Tableau and QlikView, covered next, are other kitchens with the same job.

What this page will cover

  • 1
    Data vs. information vs. insight. Why "we have a lot of data" and "we understand our business" are not the same claim.
  • 2
    The BI lifecycle. Source → store → model → analyze → visualize → decide — the same shape as the six-stage journey already taught on the Architecture page, one level more abstract.
  • 3
    Who uses BI, and for what. An operations manager watching a live dashboard is doing something different from a CFO reviewing a monthly report — same discipline, different cadence and depth.
  • 4
    Where this course focuses. Self-service BI — a business user building their own reports — as opposed to a dedicated data engineering team building pipelines nobody else touches.
