Module 1 · 6

Twelve charts. One rule: every chart answers a business question.

If you can't say what question a chart answers, don't build it. Basic charts first, then the building blocks, then the ones with more moving parts — click any card to see steps, formatting, best practices, and common mistakes. Terms like , , and are click-to-define wherever you see them on this site.

Which chart, when?

Business questionChartAvoid when…
Compare a number across categoriesBar ChartMore than ~10–12 categories
Show parts of a wholePie ChartMore than 5 categories (Rule of 5)
Show change over timeLine ChartComparing unrelated categories
Show one critical headline numberKPI CardYou need more than 4–6 things at once
Show progress toward a goalGauge ChartComparing multiple items at once
Show relationship between two numbersScatter PlotOnly one number available, <10 points
Show geographic distributionMapPrecise comparison matters more than location
Show hierarchy + proportion togetherTree MapOnly one category level exists
Show a running total moving through gains and lossesWaterfall ChartToo many steps (8+) — bars get too thin to read
Show stage-by-stage drop-offFunnel ChartSteps aren't naturally sequential/shrinking
Compare two measures with different scales togetherCombo ChartThe two measures don't share a meaningful common axis
Show exact numbers across a grid, not just a pictureMatrix / TableYou need people to feel a pattern at a glance, not read numbers
